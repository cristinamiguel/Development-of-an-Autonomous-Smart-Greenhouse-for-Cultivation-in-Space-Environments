# Development-of-an-Autonomous-Smart-Greenhouse-for-Cultivation-in-Space-Environments
Código, vídeos e circuitos relativos ao Projeto Final de Licenciatura sobre a Automação de uma estufa 
Human space exploration faces major logistical challenges as it
transitions from missions in Low Earth Orbit (LEO) to long-duration deep
space missions, such as the return to the Moonunder the Artemis
programme and the future exploration of Mars. Unlike current operations
on the International Space Station (ISS), where resupply from Earth is
feasible, a mission to Mars requires a much higher degree of autonomy due
to the large distances and long travel times involved [1].
Current physico-chemical systems are insufficient for these new mission
profiles. To make a mission to Mars viable, it is imperative to transition to
Bioregenerative Life Support Systems (BLSS). The introduction of biological
food production systems (greenhouses) is the only way to close the remaining
carbon and water cycles, transforming metabolic waste into edible biomass
and oxygen, thus avoiding the prohibitive "mass penalty" of transporting
food for a 3-year mission.
Therefore, the use of higher plants in space greenhouses plays a dual
role: they ensure atmospheric revitalisation, with CO2 consumption and
O2 production, and provide essential fresh nutrients, mitigating the
vitamin degradation of pre-packaged foods [1]. Furthermore, the presence
of biological systems offers important psychological benefits to the crew,
helping to reduce the stress caused by the confinement and isolation
characteristic of this type of mission.
